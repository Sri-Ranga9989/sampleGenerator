CONCLUSION

The European eubiotics market is entering a phase in which purchasing decisions are increasingly determined by measurable production economics rather than product category awareness alone. The market is estimated at approximately EUR 2.31 billion in 2025, with the EU-27 accounting for approximately EUR 1.99 billion. France represents approximately EUR 289 million, while Germany represents approximately EUR 359 million. Together, France and Germany account for approximately EUR 648 million of the EU-27 market.

Demand remains concentrated around poultry, swine and dairy applications, with compound feed manufacturers, premix producers and integrated livestock businesses representing the principal purchasing groups. Probiotics, organic acids and phytogenic solutions account for the largest portions of current expenditure, while synbiotic, postbiotic and combination formulations are expanding the addressable product mix. Purchasing decisions increasingly incorporate inclusion economics, feed conversion, animal performance, product consistency, regulatory compliance and the availability of application-specific technical support.

The competitive structure is characterized by established supplier relationships combined with recurring qualification and switching activity. European buyers maintain an average of several qualified suppliers, while a growing proportion of feed manufacturers and integrators operate multi-supplier procurement models. Supplier retention remains closely linked to repeatable field performance, supply reliability, formulation compatibility and technical support. Price remains an important negotiation variable, but transaction decisions are generally evaluated through cost per tonne of feed and resulting production economics rather than nominal product price alone.

France represents a particularly relevant market for supplier development because of its substantial compound-feed base, established feed manufacturing ecosystem and concentration of poultry, swine and dairy production. Germany provides another important market with a larger eubiotics expenditure base and a comparatively developed multi-supplier purchasing structure. Across both markets, procurement activity is increasingly supported by product trials, reformulation programs, supplier qualification and contract rebidding. The resulting pipeline creates opportunities for suppliers able to demonstrate quantified performance under commercial operating conditions.

For Mixscience, the principal commercial opportunity lies in translating eubiotic products into measurable customer economics. Product positioning can be strengthened through standardized performance dossiers covering inclusion rate, cost per tonne of feed, feed conversion impact, animal performance, trial duration, repeat-order economics and application-specific return on investment. This approach also creates a common commercial framework for comparing probiotics, organic acids, phytogenics, prebiotics and combination solutions across poultry, swine and ruminant applications.

The market also presents opportunities through supplier switching, dual sourcing and formulation modernization. Buyers entering contract-renewal periods, testing alternative suppliers or seeking formulation cost reductions represent identifiable near-term demand pools. France and Germany should therefore be evaluated not only through aggregate market size but also through account-level purchasing volumes, incumbent supplier tenure, qualification status, upcoming trials and contract renewal schedules.

Over the medium term, European eubiotics demand is expected to expand through greater penetration within existing feed volumes rather than through substantial growth in the underlying compound-feed base. This places greater importance on increasing value per tonne of feed, expanding application coverage and demonstrating repeatable production outcomes. Suppliers with validated technical performance, regulatory readiness, reliable supply and differentiated formulation capabilities will increasingly compete for a larger share of existing customer budgets.

Overall, the European market provides a broad addressable base across feed manufacturers, premix producers, integrators and livestock producers, with France serving as a priority market for detailed commercial development and Germany providing a major adjacent opportunity. The most actionable growth opportunities are therefore concentrated around quantified performance validation, account-level supplier displacement, product reformulation, multi-supplier procurement and application-specific eubiotic programs.
